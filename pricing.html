Why $5,000?

The honest math behind the number.

What the setup fee actually buys

The setup fee isn't IT labor priced by the hour. Building this yourself runs 80–120 hours if you're already technical — several hundred starting from zero. But hours aren't the real cost. DIY self-hosting doesn't fail through catastrophe; it fails through chronic unreliability — the sync that silently stops, the weekly restart, the system you stop trusting until you drift back to the cloud subscriptions you were escaping, because they're reliable and your project isn't. You're buying a system built and commissioned to be boring: it just works, so you actually live on it. Comparable integration work runs $6,000–$10,000 in labor alone — and the price includes six months of Guardian Care during commissioning, active refinement worth $600 at the founding rate or $1,800 at standard.

The subscription math

Most households already spend $60–$120/month renting fragments of what Trustline builds — cloud photo storage, document sync, AI subscriptions, password managers, media hosting. That's $700–$1,400 a year, forever, with prices rising and ownership at zero. A Trustline estate pays for itself against those subscriptions in roughly 5–8 years, and every year after that is pure upside — plus you own something the cloud services will never sell you: sovereignty over your own data.

Who this is actually for

If you're technical and love tinkering, you've probably already built something like this — and honestly, you don't need us. Trustline exists for everyone else: people who've wanted this level of privacy for years but were never going to spend months of evenings becoming their own system administrator to get it. The price only looks high if you value your time at zero. What you're buying is the result — privacy, ownership, and a system that runs itself — without the second job.

You can't normally buy this.

Complete digital self-sufficiency is almost impossible to purchase — because nobody with scale wants to sell it. Subscription companies are built on recurring dependence; a one-time sale that ends the relationship is against the business model. Hiring someone to manage a server doesn't escape it either — you've just changed landlords. Trustline is built backwards from all of that: our business model is making sure you never need us again. Your estate runs without us, survives without us, and is documented so anyone could take over. Guardian Care exists for households that want our continued work — improvement, not dependence — and it cancels any time without your system noticing.

Straight Answers

The questions you should be asking.

An investment this size deserves blunt answers, not marketing. Here is exactly where you stand.

What happens if Trustline disappears tomorrow?

Nothing happens to your estate. That is the entire point. Your system is built on durable, open-source, industry-standard software — Nextcloud, Home Assistant, WireGuard — running on hardware you own outright. There are no proprietary formats, no license servers, and no dependency on us to keep running. At handoff you receive full documentation, and any competent administrator could take over stewardship. The exit door is part of the architecture.

What happens when my internet goes out?

Your estate keeps working. Files, photos, cameras, automations, and your AI Operator all run locally inside your home — they don't need the internet to exist. The only thing you lose during an outage is remote access from outside the house, and that resumes the moment your connection does. Compare that to the cloud, where an outage means your entire digital life goes dark.

What if a hard drive fails?

Drive failure is planned for, not feared. Storage is mirrored, so a single drive failure causes zero data loss — the system keeps running while the drive is replaced. Behind that sits a 3-2-1 backup posture: local snapshots, off-site encrypted copies, and restore paths that are actually tested during deployment, not assumed. Every build is also stress-checked during burn-in before it ever reaches your home.

Am I locked into Trustline?

No, and we'd be suspicious of anyone who says otherwise. Your setup price includes a fixed commissioning period of Guardian Care — it's bundled in, not a recurring charge you're signing up for. After commissioning, Guardian Care is a month-to-month subscription, not a contract — cancel anytime, no penalty. Your data lives in standard formats on your own disks. If you ever want to walk away, you take everything with you, because you already have everything. Sovereignty that requires our permission wouldn't be sovereignty.

Why is Guardian Care required at first?

Because your estate is being tuned to your household. Every deployment is unique — your data patterns, your devices, your workflows, your family's use of the AI Operator. Guardian Care during the commissioning window is how we validate the system against real use, ship refinements as they're needed, and honor the Setup Week guarantee beyond Day 7. It is standard practice for any custom-built infrastructure. After commissioning, it becomes fully optional.

What if I don't want ongoing Guardian Care after commissioning?

Cancel any time. Your estate keeps running — services, storage, AI, all of it, with the Operator still handling day-to-day maintenance through its tested skills. What stops is new capability and integration work; the system itself never depends on the subscription. You are the landlord; we work for you only as long as you want us to.

Do I need to be technical to own this?

No. That is what the AI Operator exists for. It monitors system health, handles updates and troubleshooting, and answers your questions in plain English — "Is everything okay?" gets a real answer. You also receive walkthroughs and a practical operating model at handoff. You are the pilot; the engine room is handled.

Does the AI Operator send my data to the cloud?

Never — with one exception: it can look things up on the web when you ask, like any private browser search. What never leaves your network: your files, your conversations, your documents, and your data. No company logs your AI activity, trains on it, or retains it. It knows your files and your home state, but it knows them only for you. Its actions are also bounded: it does not make irreversible changes without your approval.

Why not just buy a NAS for $600?

You can, and for some people that's the right call. But a NAS is a box, not an estate. What you're paying for here is the architecture, the migration of years of data without loss, the validation of every backup and sync path, the AI Operator layer, and an accountable human responsible for the whole system working together. The hardware is the cheapest part of sovereignty. The box without the stewardship is how people end up with a $600 shelf ornament and their photos still in iCloud.

What if I already have hardware I want to reuse?

Bring it. The Sovereign Assessment includes evaluating whether existing hardware you own — servers, storage, networking gear, workstations — can be integrated into your estate to reduce cost. Not every piece will make sense to keep, but where it does, we build around it. You are the landlord; we are not here to force you to buy things you don't need.

Do I really need the AI hardware?

Fair question — the AI hardware can cost as much as everything else combined. The honest answer: the AI Operator is non-negotiable if you want digital self-sufficiency without becoming technical. Owning a server you can't run is like owning a car on an island with no mechanic — the moment it breaks, ownership means nothing. The Operator is the onboard mechanic: it monitors, explains, and fixes, so you never need to become a system administrator. And it's not something to cheap out on, because the class of hardware directly determines the quality of the model it runs. A weaker model doesn't save money — it converts money into your time, five to ten hours correcting what a capable model would have done right the first time, and it erodes your trust until you stop using the system at all. That's why we publish a hardware floor and won't deploy below it.

And if you barely use AI today: the same agentic capabilities the big cloud AIs advertise — reading your email, managing your calendar, taking actions on your machine — are fully available in the open-source ecosystem, running on your hardware, customizable in ways a cloud service will never allow. No company training on your conversations, logging your activity, or holding your assistant hostage to a subscription.

Why is the hardware so expensive?

Because the market is expensive right now. AI demand has driven DRAM prices roughly double their pre-2025 levels, and high-end GPUs remain well above their historical baseline. This affects everyone building capable local AI infrastructure — us, the biggest cloud companies, hobbyists. We source at the best prices we can find, pass hardware through at actual cost plus a flat 5% sourcing fee, and show you the receipts. As supply catches up with demand, we expect prices to normalize, and future Trustline builds will cost less. Today's Founding Members are paying today's market rate for hardware, with no markup layered on top of it.

How long does the migration take?

Typically 5-7 days, and you never go dark. We build your estate in the background, mirror your data exactly, and validate every byte against the original. You only switch over once the shadow system is proven. Zero downtime, zero data loss — that's not a hope, it's the sequence.
