Why $5,000?

The honest math behind the number.

What the setup fee actually buys

The setup fee is not IT labor priced by the hour. It is what a household pays to skip 80–120 hours of research, hardware trial-and-error, migration scripting, and troubleshooting — and to skip it with a data-loss guarantee. Comparable integration work from an IT consultant runs $6,000–$10,000 in labor alone, without the productized process or the continued relationship. The setup fee is roughly one-third to half the total price of a founding deployment, which is standard for custom integration work of this scope.

The subscription math

Most households already spend $60–$120/month renting fragments of what Trustline builds — cloud photo storage, document sync, AI subscriptions, password managers, media hosting. That's $700–$1,400 a year, forever, with prices rising and ownership at zero. A Trustline estate pays for itself against those subscriptions in roughly 5–8 years, and every year after that is pure upside — plus you own something the cloud services will never sell you: sovereignty over your own data.

Straight Answers

The questions you should be asking.

An investment this size deserves blunt answers, not marketing. Here is exactly where you stand.

What happens if Trustline disappears tomorrow?

Nothing happens to your estate. That is the entire point. Your system is built on durable, open-source, industry-standard software — Nextcloud, Home Assistant, WireGuard — running on hardware you own outright. There are no proprietary formats, no license servers, and no dependency on us to keep running. At handoff you receive full documentation, and any competent administrator could take over stewardship. The exit door is part of the architecture.

What happens when my internet goes out?

Your estate keeps working. Files, photos, cameras, automations, and your AI Operator all run locally inside your home — they don't need the internet to exist. The only thing you lose during an outage is remote access from outside the house, and that resumes the moment your connection does. Compare that to the cloud, where an outage means your entire digital life goes dark.

What if a hard drive fails?

Drive failure is planned for, not feared. Storage is mirrored, so a single drive failure causes zero data loss — the system keeps running while the drive is replaced. Behind that sits a 3-2-1 backup posture: local snapshots, off-site encrypted copies, and restore paths that are actually tested during deployment, not assumed. Every build is also stress-checked during burn-in before it ever reaches your home.

Am I locked into Trustline?

No, and we'd be suspicious of anyone who says otherwise. Guardian Care is a month-to-month subscription, not a contract — cancel anytime, no penalty. Your data lives in standard formats on your own disks. If you ever want to walk away, you take everything with you, because you already have everything. Sovereignty that requires our permission wouldn't be sovereignty.

Do I need to be technical to own this?

No. That is what the AI Operator exists for. It monitors system health, handles updates and troubleshooting, and answers your questions in plain English — "Is everything okay?" gets a real answer. You also receive walkthroughs and a practical operating model at handoff. You are the pilot; the engine room is handled.

Does the AI Operator send my data to the cloud?

Never. The Operator runs on local inference hardware inside your home — no cloud API calls, no telemetry, no training on your data. It knows your files and your home state, but it knows them only for you. Its actions are also bounded: it does not make irreversible changes without your approval.

Why not just buy a NAS for $600?

You can, and for some people that's the right call. But a NAS is a box, not an estate. What you're paying for here is the architecture, the migration of years of data without loss, the validation of every backup and sync path, the AI Operator layer, and an accountable human responsible for the whole system working together. The hardware is the cheapest part of sovereignty. The box without the stewardship is how people end up with a $600 shelf ornament and their photos still in iCloud.

What if I already have hardware I want to reuse?

Bring it. The Sovereign Assessment includes evaluating whether existing hardware you own — servers, storage, networking gear, workstations — can be integrated into your estate to reduce cost. Not every piece will make sense to keep, but where it does, we build around it. You are the landlord; we are not here to force you to buy things you don't need.

Why is the hardware so expensive?

Because the market is expensive right now. AI demand has driven DRAM prices roughly double their pre-2025 levels, and high-end GPUs remain well above their historical baseline. This affects everyone building capable local AI infrastructure — us, cloud providers, the biggest cloud companies, hobbyists. We source at the best prices we can find, pass hardware through at actual cost plus a flat 5% sourcing fee, and show you the receipts. As supply catches up with demand, we expect prices to normalize, and future Trustline builds will cost less. Today's Founding Members are paying today's market rate for hardware, with no markup layered on top of it.

How long does the migration take?

Typically 5-7 days, and you never go dark. We build your estate in the background, mirror your data exactly, and validate every byte against the original. You only switch over once the shadow system is proven. Zero downtime, zero data loss — that's not a hope, it's the sequence.
